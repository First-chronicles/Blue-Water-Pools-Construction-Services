From Dirt to Water

How is a concrete pool built?

A concrete pool goes up in four main stages. These photos come from our own job sites at each stage.

  1. 1

    Layout, forms & steel

    We dig, set the forms and tie a steel grid that gives the shell its strength.

  2. 2

    Concrete shell

    Concrete is sprayed over the steel to form the walls, floor, steps and benches.

  3. 3

    Tile, coping & grading

    The waterline tile and coping go on, then the yard is graded for the deck.

  4. 4

    Finish, deck & fill

    The interior finish, decking, lights and features are installed, and the pool is filled.

Pool Owner Tips

What should you know before building a pool?

Plain answers to the questions Southwest Florida homeowners ask most before a pool build.

Building

The 4 stages of a concrete pool build

Knowing what happens at each stage helps you plan your yard, your schedule and your questions.

Read the article

Most custom concrete pools follow the same order. First come layout, digging, forms and a steel grid. Next, concrete is sprayed over the steel to form the shell, and it needs time to cure. Then the tile, coping and plumbing are finished and the yard is graded. Last come the interior finish, the deck, the lights and features, and the fill. City permits and inspections fall between several of these stages, so your timeline depends partly on where you live.

Design

Choosing waterline tile for a Florida pool

The tile at the waterline is the first thing people see. Here is how to choose it.

Read the article

The waterline band is the part of the pool that shows the most and gets the most sun and splash. Glass tile reflects light and comes in many blues and greens, including iridescent blends. Look at samples in daylight next to your interior finish and your coping color, since all three are seen together. If you plan a raised spa, many owners carry the same tile onto the spa wall so the two read as one design.

Hiring

5 questions to ask before you hire a pool builder

A short checklist to compare pool builders on the things that matter.

1. Is the company licensed? In Florida you can check any contractor license on the state's official search at myfloridalicense.com. 2. Who pulls the permits? 3.
